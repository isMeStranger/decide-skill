# Human side of decisions

## Emotions are data, not verdicts
- Gut feelings are reliable only where you had **many repetitions with fast, clear feedback** (e.g. a nurse with 20 years on a ward). In new, rare, or noisy situations, gut is weak evidence.
- Ask what the feeling is pointing at. Dread → an unlisted risk or a value conflict? Excitement → a real upside or novelty? Add what you find to the criteria or risks.
- Strong emotion (anger, fear, euphoria, exhaustion, hunger) narrows thinking. If not urgent, wait a night.

## Quick perspective tools
- **10/10/10**: feelings in 10 minutes / 10 months / 10 years.
- **Best friend test**: what would you advise a friend in exactly this situation?
- **Regret minimisation**: imagine being 80 looking back. Long-term regret is mostly about things not tried.
- **Newspaper / mirror test**: fine if everyone you respect saw it?
- **Coin flip test**: flip for it; notice whether you feel relief or disappointment at the result. That reaction is information (you don't have to obey the coin).
- **Reverse test**: if you already had option B, would you switch to A?

## Choice overload
More than ~5 options → filter with must-haves first, then compare the top 3.

## Group decisions
1. Everyone writes their view and scores **independently before** discussion (stops anchoring and herding).
2. Lowest-ranked person speaks first.
3. Assign a devil's advocate.
4. Separate "discuss" from "decide"; say who decides and how (consensus, majority, owner decides after input).
5. "Disagree and commit": once decided, act fully; review at the set date.

## After the decision
- Tripwires: observable signals + dates that trigger a re-decision. Set them now, while calm.
- Don't relitigate without new information.
- Judge the process at review time, not just the outcome (luck exists).
